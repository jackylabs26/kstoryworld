The Meal Cheon Song-yi Loved

Language: English · Editor's Curation · K-Food

How Korean Fried Chicken and Beer Conquered the World

Photo by Theodore Nguyen · Pexels (License: Free to use)

Intro

In the winter of 2013, one line from the hit K-Drama My Love from the Star changed the global food landscape. When Cheon Song-yi (played by Jun Ji-hyun) declared on a snowy night that chicken and beer were the only proper comfort food, it was more than a memorable quote — it became a cultural movement. Chicken delivery orders in China surged overnight, and Korean-style fried chicken joints began popping up across Southeast Asia. According to a 2014 Korea Tourism Organization report, this single scene was the catalyst for the "chimaek syndrome" that swept across Asia.

What It Is

Chimaek is a portmanteau of "chicken" (chi) and "maekju" (beer in Korean), referring to the quintessential Korean pairing of crispy fried chicken with cold draft beer. What sets Korean fried chicken apart is its double-frying technique, which produces an exceptionally crunchy exterior while keeping the meat juicy inside. The variety of flavors is staggering: classic fried, sweet-and-spicy yangnyeom, soy garlic, honey butter, and dozens more regional variations. Paired with an ice-cold Korean lager like Cass or Hite — or increasingly, craft beer — chimaek is far more than a snack. It's a lifestyle. While fried chicken and beer exist everywhere, only Korea has turned the combination into a proper noun and a cultural identity.

Cultural Context

Korea's fried chicken culture traces back to the tongdak (whole roasted chicken) shops of the 1970s and 80s, evolving through the franchise boom of the 1990s that brought chains like Kyochon and BHC to every neighborhood. Chimaek crystallized as a cultural phenomenon during the 2002 FIFA World Cup, when millions gathered on the streets to cheer for the national team with chicken and beer in hand. Then came My Love from the Star in 2013, which married chimaek with the global reach of Hallyu. During the drama's run, online chicken delivery orders in China reportedly jumped by double digits year-over-year, and Korean chicken franchises raced to open branches in Beijing and Shanghai. Today, the annual Daejeon Chimaek Festival draws hundreds of thousands of visitors, cementing chimaek's place as a defining element of Korean popular culture.

How To Enjoy It

The ultimate chimaek experience happens outdoors. In spring and summer, Seoulites spread picnic mats along the Han River banks, order delivery chicken, and spend the evening under the city skyline. Pairing your chimaek outing with a visit to Han River and Namsan night view spots makes for a perfect evening. The most popular order is "half-and-half" (banban) — half fried, half seasoned — which lets you enjoy both styles. Locals cleanse their palate between bites with pickled radish (chikin-mu). You can also keep it simple: grab canned beer from a convenience store, pick up a box of chicken from any of the thousands of neighborhood shops, and find a park bench. That easy ritual is one of Korea's most accessible everyday pleasures.

Closing

What began with Cheon Song-yi's offhand craving has become one of Korea's most recognized cultural exports. Between every crispy bite and every cold sip lies decades of Korean everyday happiness, refined into a ritual the world now shares. If you ever visit Korea, find your way to a spot along the Han River and order a round of chimaek. You'll understand exactly why one drama scene was enough to start a global movement.
