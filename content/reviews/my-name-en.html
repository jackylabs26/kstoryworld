Overview

My Name burst onto the television landscape on October 15, 2021, becoming an instant sensation for Netflix viewers worldwide. This eight-episode Korean drama series presents a compelling and intricate narrative that combines the best elements of crime thrillers with character-driven storytelling. With an impressive TMDB rating of 8.226 out of 10, My Name has earned its place among the most critically acclaimed Korean dramas of recent years. The series, which comprises one complete season with eight episodes, falls into the crime, drama, and mystery genres, offering viewers a gripping experience that keeps them on the edge of their seats from the first episode to the last. The production quality, combined with stellar performances from its cast, creates a viewing experience that transcends typical crime drama conventions.

Plot Summary

My Name tells the story of a young woman whose life takes a dramatic turn following the tragic murder of her father. Driven by an insatiable desire for revenge, she makes a dangerous decision that sets the entire narrative in motion. Rather than pursuing conventional paths of justice, she places her trust in a powerful and morally ambiguous crime boss, entering into a complex arrangement that will test her loyalty, morality, and sense of self. The most intriguing aspect of this plot is that while she works under the direction and protection of this crime boss, she simultaneously infiltrates the police force—a move that places her in an impossible position between two worlds. This dual existence creates a constant tension throughout the series, as viewers watch her navigate the treacherous landscape of organized crime while simultaneously working as a police officer. The premise raises profound questions about the nature of revenge, the cost of seeking justice through morally questionable means, and the psychological toll of living a lie. As the story unfolds, the protagonist discovers that the path to revenge is far more complicated than she initially imagined, and the lines between right and wrong, law and crime, become increasingly blurred.

Cast and Characters

  • Han So-hee as Yoon Ji-woo/Oh Hye-jin: Han So-hee delivers a career-defining performance as the dual-identity protagonist. The actress brings remarkable depth to a character caught between two worlds, portraying both the hardened criminal operative and the idealistic police officer with equal conviction. Her ability to shift between these personas while maintaining the character's emotional core is nothing short of exceptional. Han So-hee captures the vulnerability, rage, determination, and moral conflict of a woman seeking vengeance, making the character simultaneously sympathetic and complex. Her performance is the emotional anchor of the entire series, and it earned her widespread critical acclaim and several award nominations.
  • Park Hee-soon as Choi Mu-jin: Park Hee-soon portrays Choi Mu-jin, the charismatic and ruthless crime boss who becomes the central figure in the protagonist's vengeful quest. His character is a masterfully written antagonist who is neither purely evil nor entirely sympathetic. Park Hee-soon brings intensity and menace to the role while also revealing glimpses of complexity and unexpected humanity. The chemistry between Park Hee-soon and Han So-hee creates a compelling dynamic that explores themes of trust, loyalty, and manipulation within their criminal relationship.
  • Ahn Bo-hyun as Jeon Pil-do: Ahn Bo-hyun takes on the role of Jeon Pil-do, a police officer who becomes romantically and professionally entangled with the protagonist. His character serves as a moral compass within the police force and represents the possibility of genuine connection amid deception and betrayal. Ahn Bo-hyun's portrayal brings warmth and integrity to a character who slowly begins to uncover the truth about those around him.
  • Kim Sang-ho as Cha Ki-ho: Kim Sang-ho appears as Cha Ki-ho, a seasoned police officer whose experience and skepticism make him a formidable obstacle to the protagonist's plans. His character embodies the conflict between institutional loyalty and personal conviction, and Kim Sang-ho brings a gravitas to the role that makes every scene involving his character compelling.
  • Lee Hak-ju as Jung Tae-ju: Lee Hak-ju portrays Jung Tae-ju, a member of Choi Mu-jin's criminal organization. His character adds another layer of complexity to the criminal underworld depicted in the series, representing the dangerous and unpredictable nature of the life our protagonist has chosen.
  • Chang Ryul as Do Kang-jae: Chang Ryul rounds out the main cast as Do Kang-jae, another character entangled in the complex web of crime and police work that forms the backbone of the narrative. His character contributes to the tension and uncertainty that permeates the series.

Instead, the series embraces moral ambiguity and psychological complexity that reflects real human nature. The protagonist's journey is not simply one of revenge but a deep exploration of identity, trauma, and the psychological consequences of living a double life. As the series progresses, viewers witness the protagonist's internal conflicts intensify, as she grapples with questions about who she really is and whether the person she has become is someone she recognizes.
