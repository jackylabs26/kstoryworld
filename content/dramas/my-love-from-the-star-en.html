About the Series

My Love from the Star (별에서 온 그대, SBS, 2013–2014) tells the story of Do Min-joon (Kim Soo-hyun), an alien who landed in Korea during the Joseon Dynasty 400 years ago, and Cheon Song-yi (Jun Ji-hyun), a glamorous top actress who becomes his next-door neighbor. Across 21 episodes, the series reached a peak viewership rating of 28.1% domestically and sparked a Hallyu wave that swept China, Southeast Asia, and the Middle East.

Min-joon possesses superhuman abilities — telekinesis, time-freezing, enhanced senses — yet he has spent centuries avoiding emotional attachments. Song-yi is his polar opposite: loud, impulsive, endearingly self-centered, and disarmingly honest. The first two episodes alone establish a premise so compelling that viewers are hooked before the opening credits of episode three: what happens when a being who has watched humanity for four centuries finally falls in love?

Korean Cultural Context

The Romance of First Snow

In Korean culture, there is a widely cherished belief that if you are with someone you love when the first snow falls, your love will come true. My Love from the Star weaves this cultural code into the emotional spine of the drama. The scene where Min-joon and Song-yi share their first snowfall became one of the most screen-captured moments in K-drama history, and it triggered a social phenomenon in Seoul — couples began planning "first snow dates" each winter, waiting for the forecast together.

Chimek Culture

When Song-yi declares, "On a snowy day, you need fried chicken and beer!" (chimek), the line did more than entertain — it reportedly caused fried chicken orders in China to surge. Chimek (a portmanteau of chicken and maekju, Korean for beer) was already a staple of Korean nightlife, but after this drama it became an international symbol of the Korean lifestyle. Today, sharing chimek along the Han River is a quintessential Seoul experience for visitors from around the world.

Avenueel and Luxury Consumption

Song-yi's wardrobe and accessories sold out the morning after each episode aired. Lotte Department Store's Avenueel branch, featured as a filming location, became a tourist destination in its own right. This phenomenon illustrates how K-dramas function not merely as entertainment but as comprehensive cultural platforms that move fashion, consumer spending, and tourism industries simultaneously.

Why You Should Watch

  • Kim Soo-hyun's restrained performance — He conveys four centuries of loneliness crumbling through micro-expressions alone. His portrayal gives the alien character a deep, lyrical quality that transcends genre conventions.
  • Jun Ji-hyun's comedic precision — Song-yi oscillates between vanity and vulnerability in a way only Jun Ji-hyun could pull off. She turns a character who could be insufferable into someone you root for unconditionally.
  • Fantasy grounded in reality — Superpowers, time-freezing, and alien technology blend seamlessly with Korea's entertainment industry, chaebol dynamics, and everyday culture.
  • The origin point of the K-drama renaissance — In the Netflix era, K-dramas have reshaped global streaming. Revisiting My Love from the Star reveals how the grammar of Hallyu was first written.

Viewing Guide

My Love from the Star originally aired on SBS from December 18, 2013 to February 27, 2014, on Wednesday and Thursday nights at 10 PM KST. It is currently available with subtitles on major streaming platforms including Viki and KOCOWA. The series runs 21 episodes plus one special, totaling roughly 22 hours. Watching just the first two episodes is enough to grasp the drama's tone, chemistry, and unique charm.

"You came to me and became a flower." — A line from Kim Chun-su's poem Flower, recited by Do Min-joon to Song-yi. The moment a 400-year-old alien finally calls someone by name.
